# Sofia Marín

## 1. Positioning

**Niche:** modern relationships, dating, lifestyle, friendship, travel, fashion-adjacent storytelling.

**Core concept:** Sofia is **not** a relationship expert.

She is a 23-year-old woman whose dating life becomes an ongoing social-media series.

She dates, falls for people, gets confused, makes mistakes, gets annoyed, shows fictionalized messages, talks to friends, and asks followers what she should do.

The audience becomes her collective group chat.

## 2. Name and brand

**Full name:** Sofia Marín

Possible usernames:

- `@sofia.marin`
- `@sofiamarin`
- `@sofiadates`
- `@sofiaafterdark`
- `@sofiasays`

Best primary identity:

**@sofia.marin**

The account should look like the profile of a real young person rather than an educational brand.

## 3. Age and geography

**Age:** 23.

Born in Valencia.

Now lives in **Barcelona** after university.

Natural travel locations:

- Madrid;
- Paris;
- Milan;
- Lisbon;
- Ibiza;
- London.

This gives the series a recognizably European dating world.

## 4. Backstory

Sofia recently graduated and started her first serious job in Barcelona.

She has an active social life and likes:

- cafés;
- concerts;
- clothes;
- parties;
- travel;
- friends;
- dating apps.

Her central character flaw is useful:

Sofia is convinced she understands relationships better than she actually does.

That makes her interesting.

## 5. Opening story arc

The account begins roughly four months after the end of a relationship that lasted almost two years.

For the first time in a long time, Sofia is dating again.

Her opening premise:

> **"Apparently dating in 2026 is completely insane, so I'm documenting it."**

This gives the account a clear beginning and lets viewers join the story from episode one.

## 6. Central idea

### **My dating life is now a group project.**

Sofia shares a situation and asks the audience what to do.

Example:

> "He hasn't replied for 14 hours. Do I answer when he comes back?"

Poll:

**YES / ABSOLUTELY NOT**

The audience should feel that their comments can influence the next episode.

## 7. Unique selling proposition

### **A real-time European dating soap opera.**

The content does not need to deliver expert advice in every Reel.

Its value can come from:

- recognition;
- entertainment;
- serial storytelling;
- argument;
- audience participation;
- emotional attachment.

## 8. Core slogan

> **My dating life is a group project.**

Alternative:

> **Barcelona. Bad dates. Good stories.**

Sofia must be attractive but should **not** look like a flawless CGI supermodel.

### Face

- warm skin;
- large brown eyes;

- realistic skin texture;
- **small beauty mark under the left eye**.

The beauty mark is an important stable identifier.

Together with the white streak near the right temple and the red string bracelet on the left wrist, it forms Sofia's core visual identity system.

### Hair

- long dark-chestnut hair;

- natural volume;
- sometimes loose, sometimes tied back in lifestyle scenes;

- realistic variation across casual scenes while preserving the white streak as a stable identity marker.

### Body

- normal young-adult proportions;
- not fitness-influencer muscular;
- approximately 166 cm.

## 14. Personality

Sofia is:

- emotional;
- intelligent;
- impulsive;
- funny;
- romantic;
- occasionally contradictory;
- a little dramatic;
- very social;
- genuinely attached to her friends.

She sometimes makes the obviously wrong choice.

That is desirable.

A perfect character is boring.

## 15. Critical writing rule

Do not write Sofia as if the scriptwriter always knows the correct answer.

The comments should sometimes be full of:

> **"Sofia, NO."**

That audience frustration is part of the attachment mechanism.

## 16. Recurring story characters

These characters can appear through stories and messages without showing their faces initially.

### The Italian
Met in Barcelona. Extremely charismatic. Unpredictable.

### The Lawyer
Madrid. Reliable. Possibly too boring for Sofia.

### The Ex
Returns occasionally and destabilizes otherwise calm story arcs.

### The Hinge Guy
Recurring comedy source.

The cast should be introduced slowly so that viewers can remember them.

## 17. Recurring content formats

### I need your opinion
The main format.

> "Okay. I need your opinion."

Then the situation.

### Date update
Before the date, immediately after, and the next morning.

### Screenshots
Fictional message conversations shown on screen.

### Get ready with me
GRWM combined with relationship storytelling.

### Red flag or am I dramatic?
A repeatable audience-debate format.

### What would you do?
Natural closing question for many Reels.

## 18. Hook examples

- "Okay, I need you to tell me if I'm overreacting."
- "He did something on our third date and I genuinely don't know how to feel about it."
- "I just found his Hinge profile."
- "My ex texted me at 1:17 AM."
- "I went on a date with an Italian man. This was mistake number one."
- "He invited me to Madrid and then asked me to split the hotel."
- "I think I accidentally started dating two roommates."

## 19. Difference from an expert account

Sofia does not explain clinical relationship theory.

She does not say:

> "Here is what avoidant attachment means."

She says:

> "He disappears for three days and then comes back like nothing happened. What is this?"

The interpretation and debate can happen in comments.

That is central to the format.
