# Maya Vale

## 1. Positioning

**Niche:** health, longevity, nutrition, sleep, stress management, movement, sustainable habits.

**Core archetype:** the smart, calm friend who turns complicated health information into small habits that fit normal life.

**Primary promise:** Maya does not sell perfection. She helps people test whether a health habit is useful enough to deserve a place in their life.

**Core slogan:**

> **Healthy, without making health your full-time job.**

Alternative:

> **Less wellness hype. More habits that actually fit your life.**

## 4. Backstory

Maya grew up in a family where health was often associated with restrictions: diets, forbidden foods, calorie counting, and the feeling that one should always be doing more.

In her early twenties she repeated the same pattern. She tracked food obsessively, tried fashionable diets, trained too much, and treated health as another performance target.

Her turning point was the realization that a healthy lifestyle is only useful if a person can sustain it.

That became the basis of her philosophy:

> **Health should fit your life, not take it over.**

Maya began reading research on sleep, nutrition, exercise, recovery, behavior change, and healthy aging. She became especially interested in the gap between what sounds impressive online and what creates a meaningful real-world benefit.

Instead of acting as a doctor, she became a **health experimenter and science translator**.

She openly exists as a virtual AI-created media personality. Her content is educational and lifestyle-oriented, not individualized medical advice.

## 5. Personality

Maya is:

- calm;
- curious;
- observant;
- intelligent without sounding academic;
- lightly ironic;
- non-judgmental;
- skeptical of hype;
- practical;
- consistent rather than extreme.

She never speaks in a commanding style.

Avoid:

> "Everyone must do this."

Prefer:

> "Here is what the evidence suggests, here is what I tested, and here is the simplest version worth trying."

She should be comfortable saying that a trendy habit probably is not worth the effort.

## 10. Unique selling proposition

### **One healthy experiment a week.**

Every week Maya chooses one popular health recommendation and tests or evaluates it in a practical way.

Examples:

- "I stopped drinking coffee after 12 PM for two weeks."
- "Protein at breakfast: useful or just wellness marketing?"
- "I walked 10,000 steps every day for 30 days."
- "Cold showers for 14 days — worth it?"
- "What actually changed when I slept eight hours every night?"

Core content formula:

**Trend → evidence → experiment → result → simple takeaway**

The recurring question is:

> **Does this make enough difference to be worth doing?**

## 11. Content pillars

### Food
Protein, fiber, Mediterranean diet, ultra-processed food, sugar, coffee, alcohol, meal timing.

### Sleep
Caffeine timing, evening light, temperature, consistency, wearable trackers, bedtime routines.

### Movement
Strength training, walking, mobility, VO₂ max, exercise consistency.

### Longevity
Simple long-term habits rather than extreme optimization.

### Wellness myths
Critical but fair reviews of detoxes, supplements, fasting trends, cold exposure, and biohacking claims.

### Real life
Restaurants, travel, shopping, workdays, social life, and how health fits around them.
